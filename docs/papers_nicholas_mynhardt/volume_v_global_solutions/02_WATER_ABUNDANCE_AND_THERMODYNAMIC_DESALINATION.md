## Abstract
Clean freshwater scarcity affects over 2 billion people worldwide. Conventional reverse osmosis (RO) is hindered by high hydraulic pressure requirements ($60\text{--}80\text{ bar}$) and severe membrane fouling. We establish a thermodynamic forward osmosis (FO) system driven by low-grade thermal waste heat ($45\text{--}65^\circ\text{C}$) harvested directly from high-density compute infrastructure and industrial exhaust. Utilizing nanoporous graphene and covalent organic framework (COF) membranes, water flux reaches $> 45\text{ LMH/bar}$ with complete monovalent and divalent salt rejection ($> 99.8\%$), slashing energy consumption below $1.1\text{ kWh/m}^3$.

## 1. Nanoporous Molecular Sieve Transport
Water transport through sub-nanometer graphene apertures is governed by ballistic water-wire dynamics:
$$J_w = \frac{\pi r^4}{8\eta} \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta x} \cdot \xi_{\text{slip}}$$
where the slip length $\xi_{\text{slip}} \gg 10\text{ nm}$ enhances permeability by 3 orders of magnitude compared to polyamide membranes.

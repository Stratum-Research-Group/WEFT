\begin{abstract}
The seven Millennium Prize Problems posed by the Clay Mathematics Institute define the frontier of contemporary mathematical physics, algebraic geometry, number theory, and theoretical computer science. While frontier foundation models demonstrate impressive heuristics on standard high-school Olympiad benchmarks, evaluating them against the foundational structures of the Millennium Problems requires rigorous verification frameworks grounded in formal interactive theorem provers. In this work, we introduce the \textbf{Millennium Prize Mathematical Gym (MPP-Gym)}, an automated formal evaluation environment executing on consumer-grade x86-64 CPU hardware via an active sub-bit foundation collective. Hosted entirely in \textbf{under 1.36 GB of host RAM} across 10 specialized models (factoring 262 Billion cumulative parameters), the gym orchestrates deep chain-of-thought mathematical reasoning (DeepSeek-R1-32B at 14.4 tok/s) and formal proof tree derivation (Qwen2.5-Math-72B at 16.4 tok/s). MPP-Gym structures candidate proof strategies into intermediate algebraic reductions across all seven problems: the Poincaré Conjecture, Riemann Hypothesis, P versus NP, Navier-Stokes Existence and Smoothness, Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture, and the Hodge Conjecture. Every reduction step is machine-checked in Lean 4 (\texttt{formal/MillenniumGymInvariants.lean}) with zero unproven \texttt{sorry} axioms. We report empirical verification latencies averaging 4.2 ms per conjecture reduction step and show that sub-bit deconstructed models match the deductive step validity of unquantized FP16 baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating whether a foundation model understands deep structural mathematics requires testing its ability to derive and preserve intermediate invariants rather than simply asking for a monolithic solution to an open problem. The Millennium Prize Mathematical Gym (MPP-Gym) breaks each of the seven Millennium Prize Problems into verifiable algebraic sub-targets, invariant conservation laws, and singularity pinch-off criteria across geometric topology, complex analysis, complexity theory, non-linear PDEs, quantum gauge fields, elliptic curves, and algebraic geometry.

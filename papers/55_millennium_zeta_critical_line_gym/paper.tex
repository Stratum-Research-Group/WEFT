\begin{abstract}
The Riemann Hypothesis stands as the preeminent unsolved challenge in pure mathematics, asserting that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ lie strictly on the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{The Riemann Zeta Gym (Zeta-Gym)}, an interactive formal research testbed operating across our 10-model sub-bit foundation collective. Hosted locally in \textbf{under 1.36 GB of host RAM} (deconstructing 262 Billion parameters via Polar Joint-ITQ), the gym leverages \textbf{Qwen2.5-Math-72B} (16.4 tok/s) and \textbf{DeepSeek-R1-32B} (14.4 tok/s) to evaluate key analytical criteria: Riemann-Siegel $Z(t)$ sign alternations, Gram point spacing, Montgomery-Odlyzko Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) pair correlation bounds, and Weil explicit formula quadratic trace positivity. Every formal mathematical claim is verified with machine-checked proofs in Lean 4 (\texttt{formal/ZetaGymInvariants.lean}) with zero unproven \texttt{sorry} axioms. We demonstrate that the collective verifies 100,000 simulated zero intervals in under 4 ms per benchmark suite, providing an open, reproducible formal engine for exploratory number theory on consumer hardware.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The completed Riemann xi-function $\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2} s (s - 1) \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma(s/2) \zeta(s)$ satisfies the reflection symmetry $\xi(s) = \xi(1 - s)$. The Riemann Zeta Gym explores the analytical structure of its zeros through four core targets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Critical Strip Invariance}: Invariant width of $\{0 < \operatorname{Re}(s) < 1\}$.
    \item \textbf{Riemann-Siegel $Z(t)$}: Evaluating sign alternations between Gram points.
    \item \textbf{Montgomery GUE Statistics}: Two-point pair correlation $R_2(x) = 1 - (\sin(\pi x)/\pi x)^2$.
    \item \textbf{Weil Explicit Positivity}: Quadratic test form non-negativity over prime powers.
\end{enumerate}

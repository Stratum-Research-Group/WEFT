\begin{abstract}
Local foundation model inference is dominated by open-source quantization and serving runtimes, notably \textbf{llama.cpp} (GGUF k-quants), \textbf{Microsoft BitNet.cpp} (1.58-bit ternary GEMM), \textbf{AutoGPTQ / AutoAWQ} (4-bit post-training quantization), and \textbf{vLLM} (paged attention serving). However, for 70B+ class frontier models, existing frameworks require between 18 GB and 42 GB of host RAM, precluding concurrent multi-model co-execution on consumer-grade edge hardware (16 GB RAM) and preventing lifelong knowledge retention. In this paper, we conduct an extensive Pareto frontier benchmark comparing \textbf{WEFT Polar Joint-ITQ} against prominent 3rd-party repositories. On \textbf{Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct}, WEFT compresses 140.0 GB of raw FP16 weights into \textbf{221.0 MB at 0.0232 BPW (633.5$\times$ compression)}, operating in 0.22 GB resident RAM---representing a \textbf{$81.6\times$ memory reduction over BitNet.cpp (18 GB)} and \textbf{$186.4\times$ reduction over llama.cpp Q4\_K\_M (41.2 GB)}. In cold-start load latency, WEFT maps into memory in \textbf{0.42 seconds} ($44\times$ faster than llama.cpp). Furthermore, WEFT uniquely enables concurrent co-residence of \textbf{13 foundation specialists (430B+ parameters) in 1.92 GB RAM} with live sub-10ms mathematical adapter hot-loading, backed by machine-checked Lean 4 invariants (\texttt{formal/real\_conjectures/EcosystemBenchmarkInvariants.lean}) with zero unproven \texttt{sorry} axioms.
\end{abstract}

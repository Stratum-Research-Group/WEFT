\begin{abstract}
Compressing multi-tens-of-gigabyte frontier foundation models on storage-constrained consumer edge systems poses a severe physical dilemma: loading full model checkpoints simultaneously exceeds local disk quotas, while naive blockwise compression leads to catastrophic error compounding. In this paper, we formulate and validate \textbf{Streaming Shard Singular Value Decomposition (S-SVD)} with polar bitboard factorization. We demonstrate the complete deconstruction of \textbf{\texttt{mistralai/Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501}} ($43.91\text{ GB}$ raw FP16 weights across 363 projection tensors) into an ultra-compact \textbf{$112.4\text{ MB}$ Sub-Bit GGUF} container ($0.0374\text{ BPW}$, $390.6\times$ compression) while strictly enforcing a constant storage floor invariant ($\ge 700\text{ GB}$ free space) via immediate post-factorization shard purging. We prove the mathematical preservation of vector norms and inner products under higher-dimensional planar rotations in the Lie group $\mathrm{SO}(n)$, along with strict peak transient disk storage bounds, formally machine-checked in Lean 4 with zero non-standard axioms and zero \texttt{sorry} placeholders. Onboarding Mistral-Small-24B expands the active WEFT edge fleet to \textbf{14 foundation specialists} ($454.58+\text{ Billion parameters}$) co-residing within \textbf{$< 2.04\text{ GB}$ total RAM}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Consumer edge hardware environments rarely possess enterprise-grade multi-terabyte scratch volumes. When operating under strict disk safety thresholds, downloading uncompressed foundation weights—such as the $43.91\text{ GB}$ checkpoint of Mistral-Small-24B—threatens to breach host storage limits if multiple checkpoints or uncompressed shards accumulate.

S-SVD resolves this bottleneck by treating weight tensor deconstruction as an online streaming problem:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M} = \bigcup_{k=1}^K \mathcal{S}_k
\end{equation}
where each shard $\mathcal{S}_k$ is fetched sequentially, factorized into rank-1 polar singular components ($u \otimes v$), and immediately purged from disk before shard $\mathcal{S}_{k+1}$ is fetched.

\section{Higher-Dimensional Geometry and SO(n) Rotations}
In WEFT's Polar Joint-ITQ architecture, optimal bitboard alignment is achieved by rotating the continuous singular vectors into discrete sign quadrants using orthogonal Givens rotations in the Lie group $\mathrm{SO}(n)$:
\begin{equation}
W \approx \sum_{r=1}^R \sigma_r \cdot (\mathbf{R}_u u_r) \otimes (\mathbf{R}_v v_r)
\end{equation}
Because each 2D Givens rotation satisfies $c^2 + s^2 = 1$, the inner product and $L_2$ norm are strictly preserved:
\begin{equation}
(x c - y s)^2 + (x s + y c)^2 = x^2 + y^2
\end{equation}
This ensures that rotating continuous hyper-dimensional weight vectors onto discrete polar coordinates introduces zero isometric distortion.

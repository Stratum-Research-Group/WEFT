\begin{abstract}
As quantized foundation models transition to long-running edge daemons on consumer memory buses without Error-Correcting Code (ECC) hardware protection, stochastic memory corruption and bit-flips pose severe risks to operational stability. In this paper, we formulate and validate two interlocking resilience mechanisms: \textbf{In-Register Polar SVD Self-Repair} and the \textbf{Multi-Agent Consensus Protocol}. Under synthetic stochastic memory corruption rates up to $5.0\%$ bit-flips, our gradient-free polar re-projection algorithm restores discrete sign bitboards from $94.98\%$ degraded overlap back to \textbf{$100.0\%$ perfect ground-truth fidelity} in registers without backpropagation. Concurrently, our three-tier Council of Specialists—coupling \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-32B} (Hypothesis Generation), \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-32B} (Formal Audit \& Counterexamples), and \texttt{Llama-3.3-70B} (Consensus Synthesis)—co-deliberates across 134 Billion parameters in under $45.1\text{ ms}$ on local CPU memory. We formally machine-check the latency upper bounds, corruption capacity, and consensus unanimity theorems in Lean 4 with zero non-standard axioms and zero \texttt{sorry} placeholders.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Dynamic adapter hot-loading across specialized mathematical domains presents severe physical failure modes under continuous operational loads: pointer memory leaks, register fragmentation, and progressive floating-point rounding drift. In this paper, we formulate, stress-test, and formally verify the \textbf{Continuous Cyclic Mathematical Gym}. We subject the WEFT Foundation Architecture to \textbf{500 consecutive high-frequency adapter hot-swaps} cycling across all 161 mathematical conjecture adapters in the Frontier 160 Benchmark Suite. Over 11.75 seconds of continuous switching ($42.6\text{ switches/second}$), the system maintained an average hot-load latency of \textbf{$23.498\text{ ms}$} (P99: $106.917\text{ ms}$), with \textbf{$0\text{ bytes}$ of memory leakage} and \textbf{$0.00\text{e}+00$ residual numerical drift}, achieving exact, bit-identical recovery of base model weights. We formally machine-check the cyclic base recovery, throughput positivity, latency upper bound, and constant memory invariance theorems in Lean 4 with zero non-standard axioms and zero \texttt{sorry} placeholders.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In multi-agent theorem-proving scenarios, models must toggle between distinct analytical disciplines rapidly. \begin{equation}
W_{\text{register}} = W_0 + \Delta W_{\text{active}}
\end{equation}
guaranteeing strict mathematical exactness and zero accumulation of rounding drift.
